\begin{abstract}
{Evaluating the subground properties during the initial stage of a foundation construction is important in order to estimate the suitability of soil quality to the technical requirements of bearing capacity, resistance to stress and strength. This study presented the geotechnical exploration of foundation intended for construction of Max IV facility of Lund University, performed in fieldwork and laboratory. The \emph{in-situ} methods included drilling boreholes, core sampling and assessment, crosshole measurements and borehole logging. The laboratory measurements were performed at Swedish Geotechnical Institute (SGI) and combined seismic measurements of drill cores, determination of the Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS) and examination of material property: sieve analysis and natural moisture content. The UCS was evaluated with regard to velocities of elastic P-waves. The synchronous light test by X-ray diffraction was performed for qualitative analysis of mineral composition of samples. We applied integrated approach of diverse geophysical methods to solve practical  tasks on evaluation of foundation strength and geotechnical parameters. This study demonstrated the benefits of integrated seismic and geophysical methods applied to soil exploration in civil engineering for testing quality of foundation materials.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Geophysical methods offer an effective tool for evaluation of physicochemical and mechanical parameters of soil in civil engineering. Information regarding the lithological structure and mineral content, geochemical and geological properties of soil can be extracted from core drilling and geophysical borehole logs \cite{MAUCEC2009194,JING2021101534}. Geophysical methods of data processing are certainly required, when non-destructive methods are advisable. Parameters of acoustic velocities of seismic waves penetrated through the specimens can be used as dataset for estimation of soil strength in the laboratory using reflectivity of P-waves \cite{Lindh202102,Lindh202210}. Data processing aimed to investigate strength, elasticity and mineral properties of specimens can be performed using acoustic imaging and seismic testing by measuring velocities P- and S-waves \cite{Tang2009,Tronicke,Hadden} as well as X-ray-diffractions for testing mineral content of soil \cite{Wang}. 

Evaluation of strength properties of soil is an essential requirement for construction works for safety and stability of buildings. Prior to constructions, soil should be reinforced using binders, which usually includes such stabilizing agents as cement \cite{Lindh2004,HORPIBULSUK20102011}, lime \cite{OKYAY201034,JAUBERTHIE2010395}, slag \cite{Lindh2021a}, cementitious materials \cite{YOON20102322} in various proportions and content \cite{Lindh2001}. Stabilization may also include environmental goals, such as cleaning the material from chemical \cite{CONTESSI2021141826,Lemenkova202222} and toxic hazardous materials  \cite{Lindh202201}. The stabilization of cured material results in the increased strength, the quality of which can be determined using uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) testing or geophysical data processing, as well as interpretation of borehole logs for estimation of soil parameters \cite{LIU2022107048,Rydenetal2016,COMINA2021106278}. The aim of such applications is the evaluation of soil strength from measured seismic velocities of P-waves of the drill core. 

Structural characteristics and mineral composition of soil can be determined from the interpretation of borehole logging using inverse geophysical methods based on correlation of density and porosity of core with the response from elastic seismic waves. Such evaluation can be performed using an integrated approach of interpretation of crosshole measurements with acoustic testing of core specimens. 

Advanced method of evaluating soil structure is the X-ray diffraction, which is an effective method for analysis of soil microfabrics, , orientation of crystal structure and identification of mineral content \cite{Denaix,Das,Righi}. 

The geologic impulse response from gamma rays in various beds of borehole enables to understand the geological nature and geophysical properties in complex sequences of drilled layers \cite{Conaway}. Gamma-ray logging of boreholes and spectrometer system has been reported for determination of mineral compositions in a drill core \cite{Ajayi} and concentrations of metals and radioactive elements ($^{238}U$, $^{133}Ba$, $^{232}Th$, $Mn$) \cite{Loevborg,Mikesell}. For instance, gamma-ray logs enable granulometric estimation of caly content, due to the high porosity and low resistivity of clay, which, together with its mineralogical composition (potassium) causes high gamma-ray emissivity \cite{Bhuyan,DIAZCURIEL2021104481}. The compensated gamma-gamma ($\gamma-\gamma$) type density is a log corrected for the negative effects of gamma rays naturally occurring in  radioactive elements ($^{238}U$, $^{232}Th$), which is used for measuring density of borehole core layers \cite{osti_5961810,osti_5614551}.

The integration of geophysical methods and geological engineering approaches for assessment of foundation properties is important, since the seismic results clearly show the correlation between the UCS and P-wave velocities which is useful for evaluation of soil stabilization. Besides, borehole logging provides with important information regarding local properties of the soil prepared for foundation construction.

\section{Background and purpose}
In this paper, we present the results of the works aimed to strengthen the foundation using geophysical methods applied for engineering geology. The drilling of borehole core was performed to select soil samples, stabilized and investigated by downhole geophysical logging and seismic methods. The test area belongs to the synchrotron radiation light laboratory Max IV (\href{https://www.maxiv.lu.se/}{www.maxiv.lu.se/}), coordinated by Lund University, Fig. \ref{Fig:01}. This facility which has a unique foundation consisting of $160,000 m^3$ of clay moraine which required to be stabilized and tested for strength. In 2010, the first sod was broken for Max IV at Brunnshög in Lund. 

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{Fig_01.jpg}\\
  \caption{Fieldwork measurements at Max IV. Photo: Per Lindh.}\label{Fig:01}
\end{figure}

The facility includes independent experimental stations, which opertates with a wide range of experimental techniques, including crystallography, electron spectroscopy, nanolithography and photo-nuclear experiments. One of the prerequisites for achieving the stable performance of the foundation of Max IV is to reduce the effects from vibrations on soil. Therefore, the work consisted of reducing the plant's sensitivity of foundation to vibrations by increasing its rigidity, so that the components in accelerators and experimental stations could move in time. The foundation also has a certain reducing effect on vibrations from the internal and external sources. The required condition for vibration level is to be below 20-30 nm RMS displacement for frequency >5Hz. 

An essential parameter for the performance of Max IV facility is related to the vibration characteristics. To evaluate the quality of foundation that should meet the technical requirements regarding the vibration, a series of simulations were carried out using geophysical methods. The purpose of the project is to study the quality of the foundation prepared for construction works and to compare it with the results from the production testing. Investigation of the properties of the entire stabilized layer enables to evaluate how well the selected production model succeeded in creating a homogeneous monolith of the stabilized soil material using core drilling, Fig. \ref{Fig:02}. 

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{Fig_02.jpg}\\
  \caption{Core drilling. Photo: Per Lindh.}\label{Fig:02}
\end{figure}

The research included the following objective: 

\begin{itemize}
	\item to achieve the homogeneity of a foundation monolith 
	\item to evaluate the strength in the bonds in boundaries between the different layers of soil
	\item to estimate the difference in density between the surface and deep-lying material through additional compaction provided by the overlying clay
	\item to test changes in material properties compared to the production samples. 
	\item to investigate the binding products on the stabilized material using \emph{in-situ} measurements in Max IV.
	\item to analyse the differences between the seismic results from the cross hole measurements and seismic measurements on the drill cores.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fieldwork measurements}
The measurements were performed in connection with the foundation works aimed to improve the quality of soil through stabilization and to evaluate the results through seismic tests. 

\subsection{Core sampling}
The field tests started with three core samples being drilled as a cylindrical sections through the stabilized layer of soil down to the underlying naturally deposited clay moraine. The drilling was performed using the S Geobor, a wireline triple tube core barrel drilling system developed by Terracore, \cite{Epiroc}. The hole bit diameter is 146 mm; the core inner diameter is 102 mm. Fig. \ref{Fig:02}. The drilling was carried out to a depth of 5 m, i.e., an under-drilling was of ca. 1 m below the stabilized layer. No core losses were reported in any of the three core drill holes, see Appendix. \subsection{Stabilization}
Stabilization of foundation is a method that works best for improving soil resistance towards the internal and external sources of vibration. Natural soil was stabilized beneath the two storage rings with hydrated lime and ground granulated blast furnace slag (GGBFS) down to a depth of 4 m. The stabilization was carried out in layers of 3 m with an undercut in the underlying layer between 3- 5 cm to ensure a homogeneous matrix without cracks and joints. The continuous quality control was performed of each stabilized soil layer. 

\subsection{Crosshole measurements}
The tests on crosshole measurements were based on the three core samples collected from the ground surface down to the bottom of the stabilized layer (to 3.15 m). The specimens of drill cores were mapped and photographed, after which selected samples were measured for P-wave velocity, compressive strength, density and deformation. Moreover, the specimens were tested at the diffraction beam tube BLI 711 at MAX-lab, Öle Römers väg, Lund. The crosshole measurements were performed with a new type of equipment with extended possibilities to measure shear wave and compression wave velocity in the material between the boreholes. The system consists of a transmitter unit that generates shear or compression waves using two receiver units (geophones), Fig. \ref{Fig:05}.

\subsection{Borehole logging}
Geophysical borehole logging has been performed using technical tools of Ramboll Group AS for detailed and precise analysis of the variations in physical parameters of core and presented in Fig. \ref{Fig:08}, Fig. \ref{Fig:09} and Fig. \ref{Fig:10}. As stabilized soil was considered sufficiently strong, no reinforcement of the borehole walls was carried out except by a thin layer filled on top of the stabilized layer, Fig. \ref{Fig:07}. 

\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{Fig_07.jpg}\\
  \caption{Crosshole measurements on Max IV. The seismic source was located in the borehole No. 1 on the far right of the image. Plastic pipes go down to the stabilized layer. Photo: Per Lindh.}\label{Fig:07}

The logging has been carried out in the core boreholes with aim to derive lithological, structural and rock mechanical information using geophysical methods. Geophysical borehole logs in Fig. \ref{Fig:08}, Fig. \ref{Fig:09} and Fig. \ref{Fig:10} also show the data for lithology analysis by a combined evaluation of the log responses and acoustic measurements. 

The borehole logging system collected information on core using probes with embedded sensors. Afterwards, captured data were transmitted the to computers using a logging cable. The system included a GPS and a depth encoder for the 3D coordinate capture. The results of the data processing include the borehole logging which represents the plots of sensor output visualised on the horizontal coordinate system against depth on the vertical axis, Fig. \ref{Fig:08}, Fig. \ref{Fig:09} and Fig. \ref{Fig:10}.

Using borehole logging system has benefits for soil testing. First, the reflected impulse of waves is directly related to the elastic and material properties of soil. This provides data on soil structure which can be received both using direct measurements and indirect evaluation. The latter includes solving inverse problems to estimate hidden parameters of soil from its related properties. 

The information is confined to a limited distance of 4.95 m tested around the boreholes drilled for Max IV. The structural analysis of core derived from borehole logging comprised data on several following parameters illustrated as vertical cross sections: 

\begin{enumerate}
	\item depth from ground level, 
	\item natural $\gamma$ rays, 
	\item inclination from the vertical level, 
	\item caliper probe 1-arm
	\item compensated $\gamma-\gamma$ density
	\item porosity calculated from density
	\item filtered radius image from the borehole wall (360\degree)
	\item 3D caliper with borehole acoustical image
	\item amplitude (360\degree) of acoustical image of borehole wall
	\item hardness computed from the acoustical median of the borehole wall
\end{enumerate}

The inclination shows the angle of vertical inclination formed between the geodetic point of measurement (located device) and the exact coordinate location of core. The inclination phenomena is owing to the physics of the Earths magnetic lines that are not parallel to the surface, which results in a slightly titled position of the compass with inclined of the needle. This information is plotted on the graphs in azimuth \degree. The caliper probe 1-arm is a mechanical device, used to measure the internal diameter of a borehole and to press a section of a probe against the side of the borehole for density logging.
	
The results of the analysis and characterisation of soil structures are shown in Fig. \ref{Fig:08}, Fig. \ref{Fig:09} and Fig. \ref{Fig:10}, which give information regarding natural gamma, calliper, acoustic televiewers, density and porosity, which have been defined by a combined interpretation of core images and acoustic images of borehole. From the acoustic measurements, the hardness of the borehole wall was calculated. The results from boreholes 2 and 3 show a variation at the depth of 2.5 m, while for borehole 2 it is also noted at 3.3 m. This variation in caliper results is also seen as a change in the hardness of the borehole wall. 

During the examination of the drill cores, a thin layer of soil, about 10 mm, was discovered that had not been stabilized. This well corresponds with the 2.5 m level. The most likely reason for such variation is that the milling down in the underlying layer was too small and therefore this layer has a lower strength. This difference in strength is not visible in all the three boreholes and therefore probably has a small extent. The difference in strength does not affect the function of the foundation at the frequencies where the construction is sensitive to vibrations since these wavelengths are much longer (bigger) than this layer.

\section{Laboratory tests}
Laboratory investigations were performed in the Swedish Geotechnical Institute (SGI) and included (i) soil analysis (grain distribution and natural water content), (ii) seismic measurements and (iii) UCS strength tests of drill cores. These investigations were based on the three core samples that were carried out from the ground surface down to the bottom of the stabilized layer. The drill cores were mapped and photographed, after which samples were selected for determination of P-wave velocity, compressive strength and density. Furthermore, material samples were tested at the diffraction beam tube BLI 711 at MAX-lab, Öle Römers väg, Lund.

\subsection{Sieve analysis and moisture content}
The deformation performance of soil as a response to stress is a complex and non-linear parameter, controlled by many factors. Mineralogy plays the essential role in strength and stress-strain behaviour of soil. It includes the porosity and ratio of voids that correlate with soil stiffness; plasticity and rheology that affect the mechanical properties of soil; structure and fabric related to the interparticle contact in soil. Since inner properties of soil influence its response to stress, analysis of soil structure is a fundamental part of foundation works.

The sieve analysis test was performed to measure the ratio of each size of grain that is contained within the tested soil specimens. The produced plots of the grain distribution curve (Fig. \ref{Fig:12}) were used to analyse the type of soil, which was detected to be moraine clays. Natural moisture content was tested in order to obtain information regarding soil properties (Fig. \ref{Fig:13}), to select a proper binder for stabilization and fine an optimal a recipe to handle the variations in clay content. 

\subsection{Seismic measurements of drill cores}
Evaluating the correlations between the seismic response of the elastic waves and strength properties of the stabilized soil is a key approach in non-destructive measurements in civil engineering. Given soil specimens are of identical type, the task is to identify the level of resonant frequency in the ultrasonic waves, such that the values of P-wave velocity correspond to the level of strength on each measured sample. Since soil is used as a basis for foundations of building, this sets up the prerequisites to achieve a high performance of ground: stability, strength and reduced effects from the vibrations. 

Seismic measurements were performed as a free-free resonant column measurement of the P-wave velocity. In this test, a cylindrical sample was placed atop of a soft foam layer to simulate free boundary environment. The vibrations of P-waves were generated by a small hammer on one end of the tested specimen and measured velocities by the accelerometer which was attached to its another end. The fundamental frequency of seismic waves was measured with a ceramic shear ICP® accelerometer. The vibrational response of the sample correlates with the inner properties of its structure and enables to estimate strength and small-strain moduli.

Measurements of the flex mode in tested specimens were also carried out. The flex mode is more affected by the boundary conditions of the specimens. It gives a higher determination of the shear wave velocity in soil sample. Comparing longitudinal resonant frequency (P-waves) and bending resonant frequency (S-waves) was performed for a quality control regarding the requirements for soil resistance to cracks and inhomogeneities in a specimen. For tested specimens, the relationship between P and S waves was shown in Fig. \ref{Fig:15}.

In case of quantitative case, the XRD is applied on reference minerals which are known to be commonly present in a given type of soil \cite{NOWAK2018133}. In our case, none of the minerals has had a known, fixed proportion which could be a function of the internal standard. This is probably due to the too small levels in relation to the high levels of clay minerals. Moreover, we note that there were no differences between the top and bottom layers which could be seen in the tested soil, see Appendix. This proves that soil was homogeneous over the tested area.

\section{Discussion}

Analysis of seismic velocities of P-wave and S-waves and their reflectivity measured in boreholes are essential methods for the evaluation of soil strength prior to construction of foundation. Besides, as demonstrated in this paper, the determination of deformation and compressive strength is possible using estimated elastic wave velocities. Our study shown the advantages of the integrated use of engineering geological techniques of borehole core sampling and geophysical seismic methods of data post-processing for geotechnical studies in industrial engineering. Measuring uniaxial compressive strength and stiffness of compacted soil is necessary to ensure that it meets the requirements of high bearing capacity and acceptable stiffness to the acting loads.

%%% 24.08

The results show a homogeneous and well-functioning stabilization that meets the design requirements by a good margin. The stabilization method has proven to be well suited for an advanced foundation in a fine-grained soil. By using the stabilization technique, the construction's concrete slab has been able to be significantly reduced with the same or better performance. In connection with the foundation works, continuous quality control of each stabilized layer was carried out. Seismic measurements were carried out both on packed specimens packed in a field and on the surface in question. Since the selected adhesive is a slow-setting adhesive, only the initial curing phase could be measured in situ. Afterwards, the curing process was measured on wrapped specimens that were stored at the workplace.

Unlike the real design, the test specimens were not stored with a successively increased ambient pressure. As stabilized soil hardens differently depending on the stress level, this process could not be captured in these measurements. The approval requirement for the test specimens was a compression wave speed of at least 1430 m/s. In Figure 3, the test specimens show an approved value already after 500 hours. The measurements made in situ showed a much slower curing process. Part of this can be explained by the temperature difference between the laboratory and those in the field. Here, however, differences in overlay pressure are added, which should favor growth in the field.

\section{Conclusion}
The results from the core sampling and cross hole measurements show good and consistent results. The stabilized foundation well meets the set requirements for a minimum shear wave speed of at least 900 m/s. Furthermore, the investigation shows that the production method has produced a homogeneous monolith without any major deviations in material properties. The method has proven to work very well with regard to the production technology, material technology and economics. No significant differences between superficial and deep-lying samples could be seen in the investigated matrix. Despite variations in the basic material.

The results from SBUF project 13152 show great possibilities to stabilize a fine-grained soil for a highly qualified foundation with very high requirements for stiffness.
